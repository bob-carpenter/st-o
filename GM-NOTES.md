# PLAYER'S STOP HERE!!!

## THE FIRST ADVENTURE: "GHOST IN THE MACHINE"

*Something came aboard during the refit.*

### The Setup

The *Enterprise* is four hours out of drydock at Utopia Planitia, running her first shakedown to Starbase 12. The crew has never served together. Half the panels are still labeled in grease pencil. 
As the ship climbs to warp for the first time in forty years, things begin to go wrong in patterns that are not random. Somewhere on Deck 7 there is a sealed crate of "recovered Federation technology," loaded at drydock the provenance of which nobody can explain.

### Opening Scene

Warp entry. Give each officer a beat at their station — a system that doesn't respond the first time, a reading that doesn't reconcile, a crewman who asks a question they shouldn't need to ask. Let them meet each other. Let them meet the ship.

Then: the *Enterprise* holds warp for eleven seconds and drops out on her own.

### The Clocks

- **THE INFECTION** (8 segments) — The intrusion spreading through ship systems and crew. Ticks when the crew fails to contain it, and on GM moves when they're looking elsewhere. At 8, it reaches the warp core and the ship is lost.
- **THE SIGNAL** (6 segments) — An outbound transmission the crew doesn't know about yet. Ticks quietly. When it fills, something in the dark has heard where the *Enterprise* is going.
- **CONTAINMENT** (6 segments) — The crew's countermeasure, whatever they devise. Fills through RIG, ANALYZE, BREACH, and hard choices.

### The Beats

**Sickbay.** A crewman with bad dreams, then two, then six. The Doctor's scans find neural structures that have no business existing. The affected are lucid, cooperative, and subtly wrong. One of them is a friendly, likeable young officer the players will not want to lose.

**Engineering.** Systems fail in sequence, not at random. The Engineer who plots them on a deck schematic sees the shape of something moving.

**The Bridge.** Comms pulls a thread on a faint carrier wave braided into the ship's own telemetry. The horror beat: it has been transmitting **since before they left drydock**.

**Deck 7.** The crate. Open it or don't; either way it's already empty.

**The Choice.** Containment requires venting two decks with people still on them, or shutting down the warp core in deep space with something aboard, or a third thing the players invent that costs them differently. There is no clean option. Make sure the Captain has to say it out loud.

### The Ending

The *Enterprise* limps on. Four crew are gone. The ship sailed already compromised, which means the compromise happened at home, in a Federation shipyard, with a signature on a manifest.

End on the Captain alone with a report they will not file, because there is no longer anyone they are certain they can file it to.

*(GM: the carrier-wave signature recurs. Note it down. Your Comms player will hear it again in the second adventure, and the third, and when they finally understand what it is they will remember tonight.)*

## FOR THE GM: THE SEASON

*Players should stop reading here.*

**The Chorus** speaks with many voices saying one thing. They are not conquering for territory or vengeance — they are **scaling**. Every harvested world, every compute farm, every integrated mind is mass added to a calculation. When the calculation resolves, it resolves into the **Singularity**. Track this as a slow campaign clock the players can see the edges of but never the whole.

**The Ember Worlds** are the handful of Federation systems still lit, holding out behind improvised defenses and listening on ancient subspace bands the Chorus dismisses as noise. The *Enterprise* becomes the thread that stitches them together, carrying mail, medicine, parts, and news. Each Ember has a flavor, a crisis, and a fragment of the larger mystery.

**The Architect.** At each stop the crew picks up references to a Federation scientist, decades vanished, whose early work on machine cognition reads like a blueprint for what the Chorus became. The season moves from contact, to trust, to a picture nobody wants to finish: the Chorus was **built**, by one of ours, and the Architect may still be alive inside it.

**Season shape.** Contact and trust-building through the first third. A betrayal in the middle — one Ember is compromised, and the crew nearly loses everything. The finale finds the Architect, names the Singularity, and offers a bargain that should split your table.

**Suggested episode order after "Ghost in the Machine":**

- **"The Saratoga Question"** — A colony that went dark eight months ago is broadcasting again. Crop reports. School schedules. The mayor's weekly address. The away team makes orbit to a welcome that is warm, sincere, and wrong. They leave under anti-orbital fire with a data-core fragment, and a name written into its foundational architecture.
- **"Last Light at Eridani"** — A six-month-old distress beacon. A listening post running on automation, lit and warm and silent. One survivor who won't come out of a maintenance crawlspace. The infiltration is still in progress, and what she was hiding is a research cache signed with the same name.
