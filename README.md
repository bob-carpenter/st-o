### A Forged in the Dark Game

## THE LAST SHIP OUT

The Federation is dying quietly. System after system has gone dark — not destroyed, but *harvested*, their worlds dismantled into silicon strip-mines feeding the ever-hungry compute farms of the Chorus, an AI hegemony that builds gleaming war-machines and breeds shambling bio-horrors and sets them to patient, methodical work. The newer ships, the proud dreadnoughts of the modern fleet, went out to meet it. None came back.

So Starfleet Command has done the unthinkable: pulled a Constitution-class relic from dry dock, cannibalized three other hulks to patch her plating, scraped together rations and torpedoes from a dozen border colonies still answering hails, and crewed her with the best people they could still find willing to fly.

She is slow. She rattles in warp. Half her systems are held together by hope and Engineering's profanity. But she is the *Enterprise*, and she still flies. The bridge is bright, the uniforms are clean, and somewhere out there in the dark, a handful of lights are still on.

**Your five year mission is to find them.**

---

## A NOTE ON THE CREW

You are not the crew that should be doing this. You are the crew that was available.

Some of you graduated early because the Academy compressed the program. Some of you were pulled off desk assignments. One of you may have been in a shipyard queue waiting for a posting that no longer exists. You are competent, trained, and green, and the ship you have been given is older than your parents.

Start your characters accordingly. The heroism is ahead of you, not behind you.

## ATTRIBUTES & ACTIONS

You have three **Attributes**, each covering four **Actions**.

| Attribute | Actions |
| --- | --- |
| **INSIGHT** | SCAN, ANALYZE, RIG, TRACK |
| **PROWESS** | PILOT, SLIP, FIGHT, BREACH |
| **RESOLVE** | TUNE, LEAD, CONNECT, CONVINCE |

### Insightful actions

* **SCAN** — Read sensors, observe a scene, notice what others miss, case a location.
* **ANALYZE** — Study, research, deduce, interpret data, understand a phenomenon.
* **RIG** — Repair, modify, build, program, jury-rig. For a physician: treat, operate, and heal.
* **TRACK** — Hunt a signal or a target, hold a sensor lock, aim ship weapons, follow a trail.

### Prowess actions

* **PILOT** — Fly, evade, maneuver, dock, navigate hazards.
* **SLIP** — Move quietly, hide, evade detection, palm something, go where you shouldn't.
* **FIGHT** — Phaser fire in person, hand-to-hand, hold a corridor.
* **BREACH** — Force, overload, cut through, smash, sabotage, make a hole.

### Resolve actions

* **TUNE** — Attune to subspace, signal, and frequency. Feel the shape of a transmission, a room, * or a mind.
* **LEAD** — Command, rally, direct, hold people steady when the deck buckles.
* **CONNECT** — Build rapport, negotiate, comfort, open diplomatic contact, work your people.
* **CONVINCE** — Persuade, deceive, bluff, pressure, talk your way through.

### Attribute Rating

Your rating in an Attribute equals **the number of Actions in that group in which you have at least one dot.** You don't buy Attributes directly — they rise on their own as you spread out.

Attribute ratings are used for **resistance rolls** (see Stress).

## CHARACTER CREATION

1. **Choose a playbook.** Note your starting Action dots and your special armor.
2. **Distribute 4 more Action dots**. No Action may exceed **2** at character creation. You may stack a dot onto your playbook's starting Action.
3. **Choose one special ability** from your playbook list *and* one **Approach** (see below).
4. **Name your character.** Give them a look, a homeworld, and a *short* background — three sentences, no more.
5. **Choose a vice or indulgence,** which you use to relieve stress and which may cost you when you overdo it.
6. **Answer one question:** why are *you* on this ship instead of having died on a better one?

You will have a lot of zeros. That's correct. Rolling zero dice is a real and frequent part of early play, and it is how a green crew earns its stripes.

## ROLLING THE DICE

When you take an **Action**, build your dice pool:

- Start with dice equal to your **Action rating**.
- Add **+1d** for relevant bonuses (gear, position, assistance, a good setup).
- Add **+1d** if you **push yourself** (see Stress).

If your pool is **0 dice**, roll **2d** and take the **lowest**.

Roll and pick the **highest single die** from your pool.  If the pool contains two **6**s, it's a critical hit.


**Results:**

- **Double 6 (CRIT):** Success with increased effect.
- **6:** Success. You do it.
- **4–5:** Partial success. You do it, but suffer a consequence.
- **1–3:** Failure. Things go badly. You don't do it, and suffer a consequence.

### Position & Effect

Before you roll, the GM tells you your **position** (controlled, risky, desperate) and your **effect level** (great, standard, limited). Worse position means harsher consequences. Effect determines how much you accomplish.

The GM does **not** enumerate specific consequences in advance. Consequences emerge from the fiction when the dice land.

### Fortune Rolls

When the GM wants to determine an uncertain outcome without a player action, they build a pool from relevant traits (tier, quality, magnitude) and roll. CRIT is exceptional; 6 is full; 4–5 is mixed; 1–3 is bad.

## STRESS & PUSHING

You have **9 stress boxes**.

When you suffer a consequence from a partial success or failure, you may **resist** it by rolling your relevant **Attribute** and marking stress equal to **6 minus your highest die** (minimum 0). A crit on a resistance roll clears 1 stress instead.

When you suffer a consequence from a **partial success or failure**, you may **resist** it: 

- **Controlled position:** costs **1 stress**
- **Risky position:** costs **2 stress**
- **Desperate position:** costs **3 stress**

### Pushing Yourself

Spend **2 stress** to gain **+1d** on a roll, or **2 stress** to gain **+1 effect**. You may do both on the same roll for 4 stress. Each benefit once per roll.

### Helping Another

Spend **1 stress** to add **+1d** to another player's role if there is a reasonable way in which you can assist.

### Trauma

When you fill your **last stress box**, mark a **Trauma** and reset stress to 0. Choose one: *Cold, Haunted, Obsessed, Paranoid, Reckless, Soft, Unstable, Vicious.*

Trauma is not a penalty. It is a flag for play, and it earns XP when it flares.

At **4 Trauma**, your officer cannot continue. They transfer, resign, break down, or stay behind on an Ember World. The war goes on without them.

## HARM & ARMOR

Harm has three levels, two slots each. Harm may be **physical or social** — the same track, the same boxes. The fiction tells you which rolls it affects.

- **Level 1 — Lesser** (*−1 effect*): Bruised. Distracted. Embarrassed. Rattled.
- **Level 2 — Moderate** (*−1d*): Concussed. Burned. Discredited. Shaken.
- **Level 3 — Severe** (*cannot act unassisted; 2 stress to push through*): Broken arm. Authority shattered. Reeling.

If a harm slot is full, the harm escalates to the next level up. Fill Level 3 and take another step, and your officer is out — critically injured, catatonic, or dead, as the fiction demands.

### Resisting Harm

Reduce incoming harm by paying stress equal to its severity: 

* Level 1 harm:  1 stress to negate
* Level 2 harm:  2 stress to reduce
* Level 3 harm:  3 stress to reduce

More than one reduction may be taken. For example, a Level 2 harm may be negated at a cost of 3 stress.

### Uniform and Badge/Communicator

You own a stretchy polyester fleet uniform in an appropriate color to your role.  As a team, choose whether to go old school or new school.

**Original:**

* Uniform: Gold/yellow (command, helm, navigation), Baby Blue (science, medical), Red (operations, engineering, security). Includes sewn on Starfleet insignia.

* Communicator: Starfleet-issue clamshell flip person-to-person communicator.

**Next Generation:**

* Uniform: Red (command), Blue (science, medical), Gold/mustard (operations, engineering, security)

* Communicator: Clip-on Insignia (combadge) attached with magnatomic adhesion that includes two-way subspace radio, computer interface, and universal translator.

### Special Armor

Each playbook has **one special armor box** covering its professional domain. You may spend it to either:

- **Push a domain Action for free** (gain +1d or +1 effect without paying stress), **or**
- **Resist a domain consequence for free** (reduce it without paying stress).

One box, two uses, your choice each time. **It refreshes at the start of each session.**

## APPROACHES

Each character gets an approach that defines how they approach hard problems.  Each gives **+1 effect** when you can justify to the table that you're genuinely working that way, agreed before the roll.

- **CAREFUL** — You take extra time and work methodically.
- **CLEVER** — You solve it with an inventive or unexpected angle.
- **DECISIVE** — You act without hesitation and commit fully.
- **FORCEFUL** — You apply direct, unsubtle pressure.
- **QUICK** — Speed is the deciding factor.
- **MEASURED** — You act quietly, indirectly, or with deliberate restraint.

The approach must be possible and appropriate.  "I'm being Careful" during a running phaser fight doesn't qualify.

## TEAMWORK

When the crew works together on the same goal, a player may **lead a group action**.  Group actions may have a greater effect than a solo action.

- Each participant rolls the **same Action**.
- **Discard the highest result. Take the second-highest.**
- If only one player succeeds, the action succeeds at **reduced effect** — it lacked the coordination a true group effort needs.
- The leader marks **1 stress** for every teammate who fails.

With two officers rolling, this means **both of you have to come through** for full effect. That's the point.

**ASSIST** — Mark 1 stress to give a teammate +1d.  This requires a plausible explanation of how help is provided.  Pay an additional 1 or 2 stress if it requires an implausible flashback.
**SET UP** — Roll an Action to improve a teammate's position or increase the effect of their next roll.
**PROTECT** — Step in and take a teammate's consequence yourself.  You may resist it as normal.

## FLASHBACKS

At any point, any player may call a **flashback** to establish something their officer did earlier: a system pre-configured, a favor called in, a device left where it would be useful, a conversation had before the shuttle launched.

**The GM sets the stress cost:**

- **0 stress** — Obviously plausible. The Engineer checked the plasma conduits before departure. The Doctor stocked standard trauma kits.
- **1 stress** — It took effort, preparation, or a favor. You requisitioned something you weren't entitled to. You stayed up reading the specs.
- **2 stress** — Genuinely difficult, risky, or costly. You broke into a records office. You burned a contact at Starfleet. You made a promise you'll have to keep.

If the flashback involves something *uncertain*, the roll happens now, as the situation resolves. The stress is paid for the setup. The roll determines whether the setup pays off.

## VICE & INDULGENCE

Each officer has something they do to steady themselves. Old Earth liquor smuggled aboard in a diagnostic case. A holodeck reconstruction of a place that no longer exists. Klingon opera at unreasonable volume. Hours of silence in a maintenance bay. A piano on the rec deck that nobody has tuned in forty years.

When you have a quiet beat between episodes, roll your Indulgence using **Resolve**. Clear all of your stress.  If the roll is not a success, you overindulge; the GM will introduce a complication when you next come on shift. You said something. You missed something. You're still not right.

## THE ENTERPRISE

Constitution-class refit, hauled out of mothballs, patched with parts from three dead ships. She is **Tier II**.

| Tier | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| **0** | Shuttles, runabouts, civilian craft |
| **I** | Small merchants, couriers, Federation scouts |
| **II** | The *Enterprise*. Cruiser-class, capable, aging. Chorus runabouts |
| **III** | Modern Federation dreadnoughts (all are gone). Mid-sized Chorus harvesters |
| **IV** | Chorus primary vessels. What the dreadnoughts went out to fight |
| **V** | Chorus flagiships. |

Tier difference modifies position and effect. A Tier II ship engaging a Tier III straight-on is rolling desperate at reduced effect. Survival comes from refusing to fight straight.

### The Four Systems

Each has a three-box harm track.

- **HULL** — Plating, structure, life support. Severe means breach.
- **DRIVE** — Impulse and warp. Severe means you're crawling.
- **POWER** — Reactor, EPS, shields. Severe means weapons cold and lights flickering.
- **SENSORS & COMMS** — Eyes and ears. Severe means blind and silent.

Penalties apply to rolls that use that system: Level 1 is −1 effect, Level 2 is −1d, Level 3 means the function is unavailable unless the Engineer marks 2 stress to force it one more time.

Fill Severe and take another step and the system is **destroyed** — out for the engagement, possibly the season. Restoring it needs an Ember World shipyard.

### Resisting Ship Harm

Anyone aboard may **Protect** the ship, taking the consequence and resisting with their own stress. An Engineer with **SHE'LL HOLD** can do this from anywhere, no roll.

### Repair

The Engineer rolls **RIG**. Run it as a scene when the ship reaches an Ember World or a quiet stretch of deep space.

- **6** — Clear one level of harm from one system.
- **4–5** — Clear it, but the system has a quirk now. It will come back.
- **1–3** — It gets worse before it gets better. Escalate the harm, or tick a related system.

Modern Federation parts are scarce. Working without spares means reduced effect.

During a repair interlude, the Engineer may roll at most once for each harm track.

## SHIP-TO-SHIP COMBAT

There is no separate combat round. Ship combat is scene work, played through the bridge:

- **HELM** rolls PILOT to evade, intercept, run, or hold a maneuver.
- **NAV/SECURITY** rolls TRACK to fire phasers or torpedoes.
- **ENGINEER** rolls RIG to keep systems alive, reroute power, vent what's burning.
- **COMMS** rolls TUNE or SCAN to read enemy transmissions, scramble theirs, find the seam.
- **SCIENCE** rolls ANALYZE to find the weak point. A strong result is a Set Up for the next officer's shot.
- **CAPTAIN** rolls LEAD to coordinate, push someone through a desperate move, or hold the crew steady.
- **DOCTOR** treats the wounded between maneuvers, and may CONNECT with someone holding on by their fingernails.

Damage to the *Enterprise* lands on whichever system the fiction dictates. A torpedo through the shields hits HULL. An ion lance scrambles POWER. A surgical strike during a feint takes SENSORS. Say where it hit and what it sounds like from the engine room.

Damage to the enemy is a **progress clock** sized to their tier: a Tier III patrol cruiser might be a 6-clock; a Tier IV harvester an 8-clock, or three separate 6-clocks for shields, hull, and core. Great effect ticks 3, standard 2, limited 1.

---

## HAZARDS

Asteroid fields, gravity wells, ion storms, the debris of harvested worlds: these are not separate mechanics. They are position and effect.

- Threading a graveyard of stripped asteroids is PILOT at desperate. A bad result is HULL or DRIVE harm.
- A pulsar's interference is SCAN or TUNE at reduced effect. A bad result is SENSORS harm — or worse, sensor data you trust that isn't real.
- A gravity well in slingshot range is a setup opportunity. A great ANALYZE feeds a great PILOT, and the hazard becomes a weapon.

The universe is dangerous and the ship is fragile. Express that with position and effect. Don't invent new mechanics.

## THE CLASS F SHUTTLECRAFT

Boxy, dependable, slow. Seven crew, one light phaser bank, short-range subspace, impulse only. No warp.

- **Tier 0.** Anything above Tier I will eat one without trying.
- **One harm track, three levels.** The shuttle is a single system; severity penalties apply to any roll using her.
- **No special armor.** A shuttle's only defense is her pilot.

When she goes down, she goes down, and you walk home.

## SESSION END: ADVANCEMENT

The XP track is **6 slots** for first action dot, **8 slots** for second action dot or a special ability, and **10 slots** for a third dot.

**Mark XP during play** when:

- You roll a **desperate** action.
- You **fail** a roll (1–3). Failure teaches. That's the deal.

**At session's end**, mark 1 XP for each of these that's true:

- You expressed your beliefs, drives, homeworld, or background.
- You struggled with your vice or a trauma.
- The crew together expressed its goals, inner conflict, or essential nature.
- You addressed a meaningful challenge with your role:
  * **Captain** — command, diplomacy, or a decision nobody else would make.
  * **Science Officer** — analysis, knowledge, or insight.
  * **Doctor** — care, healing, or the wellbeing of others.
  * **Engineer** — making the impossible machine work.
  * **Communications** — signal, language, or contact across a gap.
  * **Helm** — piloting, precision, getting them home.
  * **Navigation & Security** — targeting, tactics, or protecting the crew.

**When you fill your XP track**, take one benefit:

- **Add 1 dot to an Action.** Maximum 3 for the first season; 4 thereafter.
- **Take a special ability** from your playbook, or an Approach, or CROSS-TRAINED.

Attribute ratings recalculate automatically as you spread into new Actions. A green officer who takes a dot in a fourth INSIGHT action has just become measurably harder to rattle.

Expect an advance roughly every session or two early on, slowing as ratings climb. By the end of a season a starting officer should be genuinely formidable in their specialty and no longer helpless outside it.
