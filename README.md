## THE LAST SHIP OUT

The Federation is dying quietly. System after system has gone dark —
not destroyed, but *harvested*, their worlds dismantled into silicon
strip-mines feeding the ever-hungry compute farms of the Chorus, an AI
hegemony that builds gleaming war-machines and breeds shambling
bio-horrors and sets them to patient, methodical work. The newer
ships, the proud dreadnoughts of the modern fleet, went out to meet
it. None came back. 

So Starfleet Command has done the unthinkable: pulled a
Constitution-class relic from dry dock, cannibalized three other hulks
to patch her plating, scraped together rations and torpedoes from a
dozen border colonies still answering hails, and crewed her with the
best people they could still find willing to fly. 

She is slow. She rattles in warp. Half her systems are held together
by hope and Engineering's profanity. But she is the *Enterprise*, and
she still flies. The bridge is bright, the uniforms are clean, and
somewhere out there in the dark, a handful of lights are still on. 

**Your five year mission is to find them.**

## A NOTE ON THE CREW

You are not the crew that should be doing this. You are the crew that
was available. 

Some of you graduated early because the Academy compressed the
program. Some of you were pulled off desk assignments. One of you may
have been in a shipyard queue waiting for a posting that no longer
exists. You are competent, trained, and green, and the ship you have
been given is older than your parents. 

Start your characters accordingly. The heroism is ahead of you, not
behind you.

## ATTRIBUTES & ACTIONS

You have three **Attributes**, each covering four **Actions**.

| Attribute | Actions |
| --- | --- |
| **INSIGHT** | SCAN, ANALYZE, RIG, TRACK |
| **PROWESS** | PILOT, SLIP, FIGHT, BREACH |
| **RESOLVE** | TUNE, LEAD, CONNECT, CONVINCE |

### Insightful actions

* **SCAN** — Read sensors, observe a scene, notice what others miss,
  case a location. 
* **ANALYZE** — Study, research, deduce, interpret data, understand a
  phenomenon. 
* **RIG** — Repair, modify, build, program, jury-rig. For a physician:
  treat, operate, and heal. 
* **TRACK** — Hunt a signal or a target, hold a sensor lock, aim ship
  weapons, follow a trail. 

### Prowess actions

* **PILOT** — Fly, evade, maneuver, dock, navigate hazards. 
* **SLIP** — Move quietly, hide, evade detection, palm something, go
  where you shouldn't. 
* **FIGHT** — Phaser fire in person, hand-to-hand, hold a corridor. 
* **BREACH** — Force, overload, cut through, smash, sabotage, make a hole.

### Resolve actions

* **TUNE** — Attune to subspace, signal, and frequency. Feel the shape
  of a transmission, a room, * or a mind. 
* **LEAD** — Command, rally, direct, hold people steady when the deck
  buckles. 
* **CONNECT** — Build rapport, negotiate, comfort, open diplomatic
  contact, work your people. 
* **CONVINCE** — Persuade, deceive, bluff, pressure, talk your way
  through. 

### Attribute Rating

Your rating in an Attribute equals **the number of Actions in that
group in which you have at least one dot.** You don't buy Attributes
directly — they rise on their own as you spread out. 

Attribute ratings are used for **resistance rolls** (see Stress).

## CHARACTER CREATION

1. **Choose a playbook.** Note your starting Action dots and your
   special armor. 
2. **Distribute 4 more Action dots**. No Action may exceed **2** at
   character creation. You may stack a dot onto your playbook's
   starting Action. 
3. **Choose one special ability** from your playbook list *and* one
   **Approach** (see below). 
4. **Name your character.** Give them a look, a homeworld, and a
   *short* background — three sentences, no more. 
5. **Choose a vice or indulgence,** which you use to relieve stress
   and which may cost you when you overdo it. 
6. **Answer one question:** why are *you* on this ship instead of
   having died on a better one? 

You will have a lot of zeros. That's correct. Rolling zero dice is a
real and frequent part of early play, and it is how a green crew earns
its stripes.

## ROLLING THE DICE

When you take an **Action**, build your dice pool:

- Start with dice equal to your **Action rating**.
- Add **+1d** for relevant bonuses (gear, position, assistance, a good
  setup). 
- Add **+1d** if you **push yourself** (see Stress).

If your pool is **0 dice**, roll **2d** and take the **lowest**.

Roll and pick the **highest single die** from your pool.  If the pool
contains two **6**s, it's a critical hit. 


**Results:**

- **Double 6 (CRIT):** Success with increased effect.
- **6:** Success. You do it.
- **4:5:** Partial success. You do it, but suffer a consequence.
- **1–3:** Failure. Things go badly. You don't do it, and suffer a
  consequence. 

### Position & Effect

Before you roll, the GM tells you your **position** (controlled,
risky, desperate) and your **effect level** (great, standard,
limited). Worse position means harsher consequences. Effect determines
how much you accomplish.

The GM does **not** enumerate specific consequences in
advance. Consequences emerge from the fiction when the dice land. 

### Fortune Rolls

When the GM wants to determine an uncertain outcome without a player
action, they build a pool from relevant traits (tier, quality,
magnitude) and roll. CRIT is exceptional; 6 is full; 4–5 is mixed; 1–3
is bad.

## STRESS & PUSHING

You have **9 stress boxes**.

When you suffer a consequence from a partial success or failure, you
may **resist** it by rolling your relevant **Attribute** and marking
stress equal to **6 minus your highest die** (minimum 0). A crit on a
resistance roll clears 1 stress instead. 

When you suffer a consequence from a **partial success or failure**,
you may **resist** it:  

- **Controlled position:** costs **1 stress**
- **Risky position:** costs **2 stress**
- **Desperate position:** costs **3 stress**

### Pushing Yourself

Spend **2 stress** to gain **+1d** on a roll, or **2 stress** to gain
**+1 effect**. You may do both on the same roll for 4 stress. Each
benefit once per roll. 

### Helping Another

Spend **1 stress** to add **+1d** to another player's role if there is
a reasonable way in which you can assist. 

### Trauma

When you fill your **last stress box**, mark a **Trauma** and reset
stress to 0. Choose one: *Cold, Haunted, Obsessed, Paranoid, Reckless,
Soft, Unstable, Vicious.* 

Trauma is not a penalty. It is a flag for play, and it earns XP when
it flares. 

At **4 Trauma**, your officer cannot continue. They transfer, resign,
break down, or stay behind on an Ember World. The war goes on without
them.

## HARM & ARMOR

Harm has three levels, two slots each. Harm may be **physical or
social** — the same track, the same boxes. The fiction tells you which
rolls it affects. 

- **Level 1 — Lesser** (*−1 effect*):
  Bruised. Distracted. Embarrassed. Rattled. 
- **Level 2 — Moderate** (*−1d*):
  Concussed. Burned. Discredited. Shaken. 
- **Level 3 — Severe** (*cannot act unassisted; 2 stress to push
  through*): Broken arm. Authority shattered. Reeling. 

If a harm slot is full, the harm escalates to the next level up. Fill
Level 3 and take another step, and your officer is out — critically
injured, catatonic, or dead, as the fiction demands. 

### Resisting Harm

Reduce incoming harm by paying stress equal to its severity: 

* Level 1 harm:  1 stress to negate
* Level 2 harm:  2 stress to reduce
* Level 3 harm:  3 stress to reduce

More than one reduction may be taken. For example, a Level 2 harm may
be negated at a cost of 3 stress. 

### Uniform and Badge/Communicator

You own a stretchy polyester fleet uniform in an appropriate color to
your role.  As a team, choose whether to go old school or new school. 

**Original:**

* Uniform: Gold/yellow (command, helm, navigation), Baby Blue
  (science, medical), Red (operations, engineering,
  security). Includes sewn on Starfleet insignia. 

* Communicator: Starfleet-issue clamshell flip person-to-person communicator.

**Next Generation:**

* Uniform: Red (command), Blue (science, medical), Gold/mustard
  (operations, engineering, security) 

* Communicator: Clip-on Insignia (combadge) attached with magnatomic
  adhesion that includes two-way subspace radio, computer interface,
  and universal translator. 

### Special Armor

Each playbook has **one special armor box** covering its professional
domain. You may spend it to either: 

- **Push a domain Action for free** (gain +1d or +1 effect without
  paying stress), **or** 
- **Resist a domain consequence for free** (reduce it without paying
  stress). 

One box, two uses, your choice each time. **It refreshes at the start
of each session.**

## APPROACHES

Each character gets an approach that defines how they approach hard
problems.  Each gives **+1 effect** when you can justify to the table
that you're genuinely working that way, agreed before the roll. 

- **CAREFUL** — You take extra time and work methodically.
- **CLEVER** — You solve it with an inventive or unexpected angle.
- **DECISIVE** — You act without hesitation and commit fully.
- **FORCEFUL** — You apply direct, unsubtle pressure.
- **QUICK** — Speed is the deciding factor.
- **MEASURED** — You act quietly, indirectly, or with deliberate restraint.

The approach must be possible and appropriate.  "I'm being Careful"
during a running phaser fight doesn't qualify.

## PEOPLES OF THE FEDERATION

### VULCAN

Vulcans are a pointed-eared, copper-blooded humanoid species from the
desert world of Vulcan, famously known across the galaxy for their
absolute devotion to logic, complete suppression of emotion, and
profound telepathic abilities. Long stereotyped as cold, arrogant
intellectuals, they possess a deeply passionate nature that they
consciously control to foster infinite diversity and champion galactic
peace. 

#### Culture and Personality

- **Total Logic**: Vulcans suppress all emotion in favor of pure
logic, following the teachings of the ancient philosopher Surak to
control their naturally violent passions.

- **The IDIC Philosophy**: They live by the concept of _Infinite
Diversity in Infinite Combinations_, celebrating the vast differences
and variables found across the universe.

- **Biological Superiority**: Possessing inner eyelids, copper-based
blood, and triple the strength of a human, they are perfectly evolved
for harsh, desert climates.

#### History in the Galaxy

- **The Time of Awakening**: Centuries ago, Vulcans nearly destroyed
themselves in apocalyptic global nuclear wars before adopting Surak's
telepathic path of peace.

- **Guiding Humanity**: Following First Contact, Vulcans spent a
century acting as cautious mentors to Earth, holding back warp
technology until they deemed humans stable.

- **The Romulan Schism**: Their history is deeply tied to the
  Romulans, a sister species who rejected logic and split off into an
  aggressive space empire. 
 
#### Special Abilities

- **MIND MELD** — Touch a sentient mind and share it. Ask three true
  questions, or convey something words cannot carry. Mark 2
  stress. What you take, you keep. 
- **NERVE PINCH** — You can render a humanoid unconscious silently,
  without harm and without a roll, if you can reach their shoulder
  unopposed. 
- **CONTROL** — You may choose not to feel a consequence. Once per
  scene, ignore the effect of a Level 1 harm or clear 1 stress. It
  surfaces later, and it will be worse. 

### KLINGON

Klingons are a ridge-headed, imposing humanoid species from the planet
Qo'noS, famously known across the galaxy for their feudal empire,
uncompromising warrior ethos, and relentless pursuit of personal honor
in battle. Long stereotyped as primitive, bloodthirsty conquerors,
they possess a complex political structure and a rich, poetic
tradition rooted in loyalty, family houses, and spiritual destiny.

#### Culture and Personality

- **Warrior Code**: Klingon culture revolves entirely around personal
honor, strength, and glory achieved through hand-to-hand combat.

- **The Great Houses**: Their society is organized into a feudal
system of noble, competing families who vie for political power within
the High Council.

- **Sto-vo-kor**: They believe that warriors who die honorably in
battle ascend to a warrior paradise, while cowards are sent to
Gre'thor.

#### History in the Galaxy

- **The Cold War Era**: Throughout the 23rd century, the Klingon
Empire was the primary military rival to the Federation, clashing
frequently along borders.

- **The Khitomer Accords**: Driven by the catastrophic explosion of
their moon Praxis, Chancellor Gorkon initiated peace talks that
resulted in a lasting alliance with Earth.

- **Galactic Defenders**: Klingon forces played a critical, bloody
  role in saving the Alpha Quadrant during the Dominion War alongside
  Starfleet and the Romulans. 

#### Special Abilities

- **BLOOD SINGS** — When you are the last one standing, or the only
  one still moving toward the objective, +1d and +1 effect until
  someone reaches you. 
- **REDUNDANT ANATOMY** — Your second heart, third lung, and
  eight-chambered spine mean something. Downgrade the first Level 2
  harm you take each session to Level 1. 
- **BAT'LETH DRILL** — +1d to FIGHT with a blade. You brought one
  aboard. Nobody has successfully asked you to stow it. 

### HUMAN

Humans are a highly adaptable, diverse humanoid species from the
planet Earth, famously known across the galaxy for their insatiable
curiosity, emotional volatility, and unique genius for uniting
fractured civilizations. Long stereotyped as reckless, fast-moving
pioneers by older empires, they possess an uncanny diplomatic drive
and an unpredictable resilience that made them the primary architects
of interstellar unity.

#### Culture and Personality

- **Unpredictable Adaptability**: Humans are defined by their intense
curiosity, emotional volatility, and an uncanny ability to unite
wildly different alien cultures.

- **The Pioneer Spirit**: Driven by a desire to look past the next
horizon, they favor exploration, scientific advancement, and peaceful
diplomacy over military conquest.

- **Rapid Evolution**: Unlike older, slower-moving galactic empires,
Humans progressed from global devastation to a peaceful interstellar
superpower in just one century.

#### History in the Galaxy

- **World War III**: Earth nearly collapsed in the mid-21st century
due to nuclear conflict and genetic engineering wars that wiped out
major global governments.

- **The Phoenix Flight**: In 2063, scientist Zefram Cochrane launched
Earth's first warp-capable ship, attracting a passing Vulcan vessel
and changing human history forever.

- **Architects of Unity**: Humans became the primary political drivers
  behind the creation of Starfleet and the Federation, turning former
  enemies into trusted allies. 

#### Special Abilities

- **ADAPTABLE** — Once per session, roll an Action you have zero dots
  in as though you had one. You have been in a version of this before,
  or you're a fast learner, or you're lying convincingly to yourself. 
- **IMPROVISE** — +1d when your plan has visibly fallen apart and
  you're building the next one out loud. 
- **TOO MANY FRIENDS** — Once per session, name someone you know on a
  station, colony, or ship you've just arrived at. They're
  real. Whether they're glad to see you is the GM's call. 

### FERENGI

Ferengi are a large-eared, bronze-skinned humanoid species from
Ferenginar, famously known across the galaxy for their
hyper-capitalist trade networks, relentless pursuit of profit, and
unique adherence to the Rules of Acquisition. Long stereotyped as
greedy, underhanded scavengers, they possess an unmatched mathematical
brilliance and an opportunistic adaptability that eventually fueled
massive progressive social reforms.

#### Culture and Personality

- **Hyper-Capitalism**: Ferengi culture centers entirely on commerce,
profit, and the accumulation of wealth as the ultimate measure of
personal worth.

- **Rules of Acquisition**: They govern their lives and businesses
using a sacred code of 285 aphorisms detailing how to successfully
exploit others for financial gain.

- **Societal Evolution**: Traditionally deeply patriarchal and
restrictive toward women, their society underwent massive progressive
reforms under Grand Nagus Rom in the late 24th century.

#### History in the Galaxy

- **The Rules of Engagement**: First making formal contact with
Starfleet in 2364, they initially acted as unpredictable raiders
before settling into an opportunistic trading empire.

- **The Dominion War**: Though officially neutral, the Ferengi
Alliance surreptitiously aided the Federation, recognizing that a
Dominion victory would destroy free trade.

- **Deep Space 9**: Their culture is famously explored through Quark,
  Nog, and Rom on the Official Deep Space Nine Platform, showing their
  transition from isolated capitalists to progressive galactic
  citizens. 

#### Special Abilities 

- **APPRAISAL** — You know what anything is worth to whoever wants it,
  including information, safe passage, and people. Ask the GM; the
  answer is true.
- **LOBES** — +1 effect on CONNECT or CONVINCE in any negotiation
  where both sides want something. You hear the give in a voice before
  they know it's there.
- **CONTINGENCY FUND** — Once per session, produce a bribe, a trade
  good, or a component you absolutely should not still have. Don't ask.
- **THE RULES** — Cite a Rule of Acquisition that applies. If the
  table agrees it fits, +1d. Each Rule works once per session.

### ANDORIAN

Andorians are a blue-skinned, antennaed humanoid species from the icy
moon of Andoria, famously known across the galaxy for their passionate
emotional depth, militaristic honor codes, and formidable warrior
heritage. Long stereotyped as volatile and suspicious combatants, they
possess a profound capacity for loyalty and a disciplined strength
that transformed them into legendary defenders of peace.

#### Culture and Personality

- **Passionate and Guarded**: Andorians are highly emotional, intensely
loyal, and deeply protective of their families, or foursomes, due to a
complex four-gender mating system.

- **Warrior Ethos**: Driven by a militaristic code of honor, they view
combat readiness as a necessity but value disciplined strength over
reckless violence.

- **Biological Antennas**: Their distinct cranial antennas act as
sensory organs for balance and tracking, reacting visually to their
emotional states.

#### History 

- **The Imperial Guard**: In the 22nd century, their military arm
clashed frequently with the Vulcan High Command over border disputes
and the surveillance outpost of P'Jem.

- **Founding the Federation**: Mutual trust forged between Commander
Shran and Captain Jonathan Archer paved the way for Andoria to
co-found the Federation in 2161.

- **Sub-Species Ties**: They share their icy homeworld of Andoria with
  the Aenar, a blind, telepathic, and strictly pacifist sister
  species. 

#### Special Abilities

- **ANTENNAE** — You sense direction, air movement, and the presence
  of bodies in total darkness. +1d to SCAN or TRACK without light. 
- **KEEN COLD** — Cold environments never reduce your effect. Heat is
  a different problem.
- **DUELING TRADITION** — +1d to FIGHT one clearly named opponent, and
  you may not disengage until it's settled. 

### TELLARITE

Tellarites are a stout, pig-nosed humanoid species from Tellar Prime,
famously known across the galaxy for their abrasive diplomacy,
stubborn demeanor, and unique passion for aggressive verbal
debate. Long stereotyped as mere argumentative contrarians, they
possess a fiercely loyal nature and a deep engineering brilliance that
built the backbone of early Starfleet infrastructure.

#### Culture and Personality

- **Argumentative Nature**: Tellarites thrive on verbal sparring,
viewing heated arguments and introductory insults as polite
conversation and a sport.

- **Skilled Engineers**: They possess a natural aptitude for heavy
industry, mining, and large-scale starship construction.

- **Honest Diplomacy*: Despite their abrasive and stubborn demeanor,
they are deeply honest, transparent, and fierce loyalists once an
alliance is forged.

#### History in the Galaxy

- **Early Conflicts**: Before achieving warp capability and expanding
into space, they survived destructive ideological conflicts on their
homeworld known as the Voice Wars.

- **The Coalition and Federation**: Mid-22nd-century trade disputes
with Vulcans and Earth eventually gave way to cooperation, culminating
in the signing of the Federation Charter.

- **Industrial Backbone**: Their robust ship designs and manufacturing
  power became critical assets to the early Starfleet infrastructure. 

#### Special Abilities 

- **ARGUMENT AS ART** — +1 effect on CONVINCE when you open by
  insulting the person. Among your people, this is respect. 
- **BUILT LIKE THE HOMEWORLD** — +1d to any roll of pure physical
  endurance or load-bearing stubbornness. 
- **NOSE FOR NONSENSE** — You know when someone is bluffing, though
  not what the truth is. 

### BETAZOID

Betazoids are a dark-eyed, indistinguishable-from-human humanoid
species from the paradisiacal world of Betazed, famously known across
the galaxy for their natural telepathic abilities, profound emotional
empathy, and completely transparent social customs. Long stereotyped
as intrusive mind-readers or overly eccentric diplomats, they possess
a deeply peaceful culture and an unparalleled psychological insight
that makes them invaluable advisors throughout the Federation.

#### Culture and Personality

- **Natural Telepathy**: Betazoids are born with powerful telepathic
minds, communicating effortlessly through thought and completely
outgrowing vocal speech when interacting among their own kind.

- **Radical Transparency**: Because they can read minds and emotions,
their society has zero secrets, leading to a culture of absolute
honesty, no privacy taboos, and completely nude wedding ceremonies.

- **Empathic Counseling**: Their innate ability to feel the exact
emotions of others makes them world-class psychologists, diplomats,

### El-Aurians

El-Aurians are a long-lived, physically human-like species from an

both comforting confidants and dangerous adversaries to god-like

### Denobulans

Denobulans are a ridge-faced, highly expressive humanoid species from
the planet Denobula Prime, famously known across the galaxy for their
intensely complex polyamorous family structures, vibrant social

peaceful, collaborative civilization that values patience,
intellectual pursuit, and a healthy balance between crowded communal
living and solitary hibernation.

#### Culture and Personality

agility.

annual hibernation cycle lasting five to six days, during which waking

#### History in the Galaxy

enlightenment that successfully united their crowded homeworld without

### HOLOGRAM

Photonic Lifeforms (commonly referred to as Holograms) are sentient,
artificial entities constructed from light, magnetic fields, and

resilience that challenges the very definition of organic life.

#### Culture and Personality

- **Adaptive Programming**: Holograms possess a unique cognitive
matrix that expands beyond their baseline subroutines, allowing them
to accumulate life experiences, form genuine emotions, and develop
distinct personalities.

- **The Struggle for Rights**: Lacking a traditional homeworld,
sentient holograms share a unified cultural movement focused on
achieving legal recognition as individual persons rather than
corporate property.

- **Environmental tethering**: Their existence is completely dependent

mobile technology to maintain a physical presence and interact with
the physical world.

#### History in the Galaxy

- **The Voyager Precedent**: Marooned in the Delta Quadrant, the

  system failures occasionally triggered deep existential conflicts,
  forcing organic societies to fundamentally rethink the moral

- **PHOTONIC** — You are not affected by vacuum, atmosphere,

## GENERAL ABILITIES

Available to any playbook, any species.

- **VETERAN** — You served on a ship that didn't come back. Once per
  session, take +1d on a roll where that experience applies, and say
  one true thing about it. 
- **STEADY** — Your stress track is 10 boxes instead of 9.
- **TOUGH** — The first Level 1 harm you take each session is
  downgraded to nothing. 
- **QUICK STUDY** — After watching someone do something once, take +1d
  the first time you attempt it yourself. 
- **IMPROVISED WEAPON** — Anything in your hands counts as armed. +1
  effect to FIGHT with something not designed for it. 
- **SECOND WIND** — Once per session, when you would mark trauma,
  instead clear 3 stress and describe what steadies you. 

## TEAMWORK

When the crew works together on the same goal, a player may **lead a
group action**.  Group actions may have a greater effect than a solo
action. 

- Each participant rolls the **same Action**.
- **Discard the highest result. Take the second-highest.**
- If only one player succeeds, the action succeeds at **reduced
  effect** — it lacked the coordination a true group effort needs. 
- The leader marks **1 stress** for every teammate who fails.

With two officers rolling, this means **both of you have to come
through** for full effect. That's the point. 

**ASSIST** — Mark 1 stress to give a teammate +1d.  This requires a
plausible explanation of how help is provided.  Pay an additional 1 or
2 stress if it requires an implausible flashback. 
**SET UP** — Roll an Action to improve a teammate's position or
increase the effect of their next roll. 
**PROTECT** — Step in and take a teammate's consequence yourself.  You
may resist it as normal.

## FLASHBACKS

At any point, any player may call a **flashback** to establish
something their officer did earlier: a system pre-configured, a favor
called in, a device left where it would be useful, a conversation had
before the shuttle launched. 

**The GM sets the stress cost:**

- **0 stress** — Obviously plausible. The Engineer checked the plasma
  conduits before departure. The Doctor stocked standard trauma kits. 
- **1 stress** — It took effort, preparation, or a favor. You
  requisitioned something you weren't entitled to. You stayed up
  reading the specs. 
- **2 stress** — Genuinely difficult, risky, or costly. You broke into
  a records office. You burned a contact at Starfleet. You made a
  promise you'll have to keep. 

If the flashback involves something *uncertain*, the roll happens now,
as the situation resolves. The stress is paid for the setup. The roll
determines whether the setup pays off.

## VICE & INDULGENCE

Each officer has something they do to steady themselves. Old Earth
liquor smuggled aboard in a diagnostic case. A holodeck reconstruction
of a place that no longer exists. Klingon opera at unreasonable
volume. Hours of silence in a maintenance bay. A piano on the rec deck
that nobody has tuned in forty years. 

When you have a quiet beat between episodes, roll your Indulgence
using **Resolve**. Clear all of your stress.  If the roll is not a
success, you overindulge; the GM will introduce a complication when
you next come on shift. You said something. You missed
something. You're still not right.

## THE ENTERPRISE

Constitution-class refit, hauled out of mothballs, patched with parts
from three dead ships. She is **Tier II**. 

| Tier | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| **0** | Shuttles, runabouts, civilian craft |
| **I** | Small merchants, couriers, Federation scouts |
| **II** | The *Enterprise*. Cruiser-class, capable, aging. Chorus runabouts |
| **III** | Modern Federation dreadnoughts (all are gone). Mid-sized Chorus harvesters |
| **IV** | Chorus primary vessels. What the dreadnoughts went out to fight |
| **V** | Chorus flagiships. |

Tier difference modifies position and effect. A Tier II ship engaging
a Tier III straight-on is rolling desperate at reduced
effect. Survival comes from refusing to fight straight. 

### The Four Systems

Each has a three-box harm track.

- **HULL** — Plating, structure, life support. Severe means breach.
- **DRIVE** — Impulse and warp. Severe means you're crawling.
- **POWER** — Reactor, EPS, shields. Severe means weapons cold and lights flickering.
- **SENSORS & COMMS** — Eyes and ears. Severe means blind and silent.

Penalties apply to rolls that use that system: Level 1 is −1 effect,
Level 2 is −1d, Level 3 means the function is unavailable unless the
Engineer marks 2 stress to force it one more time. 

Fill Severe and take another step and the system is **destroyed** —
out for the engagement, possibly the season. Restoring it needs an
Ember World shipyard. 

### Resisting Ship Harm

Anyone aboard may **Protect** the ship, taking the consequence and
resisting with their own stress. An Engineer with **SHE'LL HOLD** can
do this from anywhere, no roll. 
 
### Repair

The Engineer rolls **RIG**. Run it as a scene when the ship reaches an
Ember World or a quiet stretch of deep space. 

- **6** — Clear one level of harm from one system.
- **4:5** — Clear it, but the system has a quirk now. It will come back.
- **1–3** — It gets worse before it gets better. Escalate the harm, or
  tick a related system. 

Modern Federation parts are scarce. Working without spares means reduced effect.

During a repair interlude, the Engineer may roll at most once for each harm track.

## SHIP-TO-SHIP COMBAT

There is no separate combat round. Ship combat is scene work, played
through the bridge: 

- **HELM** rolls PILOT to evade, intercept, run, or hold a maneuver.
- **NAV/SECURITY** rolls TRACK to fire phasers or torpedoes. 
- **ENGINEER** rolls RIG to keep systems alive, reroute power, vent
  what's burning. 
- **COMMS** rolls TUNE or SCAN to read enemy transmissions, scramble
  theirs, find the seam. 
- **SCIENCE** rolls ANALYZE to find the weak point. A strong result is
  a Set Up for the next officer's shot. 
- **CAPTAIN** rolls LEAD to coordinate, push someone through a
  desperate move, or hold the crew steady. 
- **DOCTOR** treats the wounded between maneuvers, and may CONNECT
  with someone holding on by their fingernails. 

Damage to the *Enterprise* lands on whichever system the fiction
dictates. A torpedo through the shields hits HULL. An ion lance
scrambles POWER. A surgical strike during a feint takes SENSORS. Say
where it hit and what it sounds like from the engine room. 

Damage to the enemy is a **progress clock** sized to their tier: a
Tier III patrol cruiser might be a 6-clock; a Tier IV harvester an
8-clock, or three separate 6-clocks for shields, hull, and core. Great
effect ticks 3, standard 2, limited 1.

## HAZARDS

Asteroid fields, gravity wells, ion storms, the debris of harvested
worlds: these are not separate mechanics. They are position and
effect. 

- Threading a graveyard of stripped asteroids is PILOT at desperate. A
  bad result is HULL or DRIVE harm. 
- A pulsar's interference is SCAN or TUNE at reduced effect. A bad
  result is SENSORS harm — or worse, sensor data you trust that isn't
  real. 
- A gravity well in slingshot range is a setup opportunity. A great
  ANALYZE feeds a great PILOT, and the hazard becomes a weapon. 

The universe is dangerous and the ship is fragile. Express that with
position and effect. Don't invent new mechanics.

## THE CLASS F SHUTTLECRAFT

Boxy, dependable, slow. Seven crew, one light phaser bank, short-range
subspace, impulse only. No warp. 

- **Tier 0.** Anything above Tier I will eat one without trying.
- **One harm track, three levels.** The shuttle is a single system;
  severity penalties apply to any roll using her. 
- **No special armor.** A shuttle's only defense is her pilot.

When she goes down, she goes down, and you walk home.

## SESSION END: ADVANCEMENT

The XP track is **6 slots** for first action dot, **8 slots** for
second action dot or a special ability, and **10 slots** for a third
dot. 

**Mark XP during play** when:

- You roll a **desperate** action.
- You **fail** a roll (1–3). Failure teaches. That's the deal.

**At session's end**, mark 1 XP for each of these that's true:

- You expressed your beliefs, drives, homeworld, or background.
- You struggled with your vice or a trauma.
- The crew together expressed its goals, inner conflict, or essential
  nature. 
- You addressed a meaningful challenge with your role:
  * **Captain** — command, diplomacy, or a decision nobody else would
    make. 
  * **Science Officer** — analysis, knowledge, or insight.
  * **Doctor** — care, healing, or the wellbeing of others.
  * **Engineer** — making the impossible machine work.
  * **Communications** — signal, language, or contact across a gap.
  * **Helm** — piloting, precision, getting them home.
  * **Navigation & Security** — targeting, tactics, or protecting the
    crew. 

**When you fill your XP track**, take one benefit:

- **Add 1 dot to an Action.** Maximum 3 for the first season; 4
  thereafter. 
- **Take a special ability** from your playbook, or an Approach, or
  CROSS-TRAINED. 

Attribute ratings recalculate automatically as you spread into new
Actions. A green officer who takes a dot in a fourth INSIGHT action
has just become measurably harder to rattle. 

Expect an advance roughly every session or two early on, slowing as
ratings climb. By the end of a season a starting officer should be
genuinely formidable in their specialty and no longer helpless outside
it.
